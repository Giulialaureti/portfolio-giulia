04 UX/UI · Web development · 2024

An Editorial E-commerce Platform

From a magazine to a digital ecosystem.

Context
Magazine, blog, community and e-commerce
Role
UX/UI Designer and Web Developer
Activities
Requirements, architecture, interfaces, development, launch and maintenance
Technology
WordPress, Elementor, e-commerce and Site Kit

In brief

The editorial e-commerce platform needed to strengthen its digital presence and turn an editorial heritage into an experience that could be browsed, updated and purchased online. Within a working team, I covered UX, UI and site development, from client discussions through to launch and day-to-day management.

The challenge and the goals

The site had to bring together information, discovery of the local area, subscriptions and participation. The goals agreed with the client were to showcase articles, guides and villages; support products and subscriptions; let the editorial team publish independently; and open the site up to comments, reviews and external content.

Free and premium content guided users from discovery through to buying single issues or subscriptions, following an architecture reconstructed from the project materials:

Home→ Magazine→ Guide→ Blog→ Publications→ Subscribers→ Contact

My contribution

I covered UX, UI and site development within a working team, from client discussions through to launch and day-to-day management in WordPress.

  • 01 · Requirements

    Meetings with the client and analysis of editorial and commercial needs.

  • 02 · Architecture

    Navigation map, sections, hierarchies and paths to content and purchase.

  • 03 · UX and UI

    Wireframes, mockups and a responsive interface consistent with the editorial identity.

  • 04 · Development

    WordPress, blog, e-commerce, comments and reviews.

  • 05 · Launch

    Testing, publishing, access configuration, privacy and management tools.

  • 06 · Evolution

    Updates, new products, user support and monitoring with Site Kit.

Key decisions

Integrating commerce and editorial voice

E-commerce wasn't treated as a separate area. Images, typography and hierarchies kept the magazine's character, while price, frequency, format and the purchase action stayed immediately readable.

Connecting the magazine, guide and community

The architecture brought together editorial content, publications, subscriptions, initiatives and partnerships. The community section made benefits and collaborations visible without interrupting access to the main content.

Designing for continuity

After launch, the work continued with editorial and commercial updates, new products, access management, privacy configuration, support and monitoring through Site Kit.

Outcome and limitations

A complete editorial and e-commerce platform was published. The structure set up during the engagement remained recognisable in the site's later evolutions.

The original version is no longer online and the initial wireframes aren't available. The homepage shown in this portfolio is therefore a demonstrative reconstruction, built from original screenshots, assets and requirements; it is not a screenshot of the published site. The available Analytics data covers a limited period and documents only the activation of monitoring, without yet reflecting growth over time.
